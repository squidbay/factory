# STAGES — growing your factory, one celebrated step at a time

**For the human: you never do more than one stage at a sitting, and each one ends with something real working.** For the seat running this (usually Engineer, at first contact): one instruction at a time, wait for "done," then the next — never a menu. Every stage closes with a genuine celebration and a journal entry. There is no deadline; the factory idles safely between stages.

---

## Stage 0 — First contact 🎉

*You've attached your new repo to Claude Code, typed one word to wake it, and the team came to life.*

- **The trigger is one word.** After you attach the repo you'll see an empty message box, not a greeting — Claude Code waits for you to speak first. Type **`hi`** (or anything — "hello", "now what", "let's go") and press enter. That first message wakes the team; you can't get it wrong.
- Engineer introduces the team briefly — **four seats: Coach, Team Leader, Engineer, Creative Director** — and asks the only question that matters: **"What are we building?"** Your answer picks a [mission](../missions/README.md), and that mission becomes the factory's first real work — this whole system exists to build *your* thing, not to be admired.
- **The team arrives already named** — an anchor for your factory and a mark for every seat, filled in before you got here, so nothing waits on you. If you'd rather they were something else, say so whenever you like: renaming is a ten-second PR, any day, forever.
- Engineer also asks, once, how you want the rest of the setup to go: **"set it all up for me"** (Engineer sequences every remaining step and — once Claude in Chrome is installed — drives the browser through them itself, pausing only where a dialog needs your hand) or **"set it up with me"** (one instruction at a time, like now). Both answers are right, you can switch any time, and the full click-map either way — every dialog named, honest gaps included — is [`SETUP-PATH.md`](SETUP-PATH.md).
- Engineer opens the factory's first PR: a journal entry *about you* — what you're building and why — plus any roster tweak you asked for. (If you spot a small "Generated by Claude Code" line at the bottom of the proposal, that's the tool signing its work — expected, and harmless.)
- **You merge it.** That's the whole core mechanic of the factory, learned by doing it once: nothing becomes real until you click the button.

*Done looks like: your project written into the journal, your first merge behind you.*

## Stage 1 — The gate 🎉

> **The full connection map — every grant, every gate, the mini-probe for each, and the one shared token that gives your chat rooms their hands — lives in [`FIRST-CONTACT.md`](FIRST-CONTACT.md).** Read it if you want the whole picture; the steps below are the guided path through it.

*Seeing with your own eyes that nothing moves without you.*

- First, the honest map of the ground. Your gate holds because **every seat is bound to it**: no seat ever pushes to `main` — every change arrives as a pull request you merge, on any GitHub plan, from day one (RULE 14). GitHub can *additionally* lock that rule into the platform itself with **branch protection** — and that enforcement is free on **public** repos, so your workshop can have it the day it exists. On a **private** repo like your office, GitHub Free lets you create the setting but doesn't enforce it until GitHub Pro — so think of the platform lock as an upgrade waiting on the shelf, not a missing piece. The clicks, and the fuller plain-words picture, live in [`hosting/github/`](../hosting/github/README.md).
- Then the test you can run on **any** plan — and the real point of this stage: Engineer changes something the only way it ever will. It opens a one-line PR instead of touching `main`, and you watch the whole discipline work: the proposal, the explanation, your click, and only then the change.
- **Flip the toggle that makes template updates work.** While you're in your repo's settings, turn on the one switch that lets your factory pull its own template updates later — GitHub ships it **off**, and with it off your first update run fails at the step that opens its PR. The full click-path and the exact failure are named in [`SETUP-PATH.md`](SETUP-PATH.md) §E; do it now, once, and your first `factory-update` run opens its PR instead of stalling on it.

*Done looks like: you watched a seat propose instead of push; your second merge behind you.*

## Stage 2 — The center 🎉

*Your team leader comes online.*

- Engineer walks you through the two one-time setups, click by click. First, **the GitHub connection ritual — the whole ceremony, and the only time you'll do it:** in the chat, tap **+** → connect **GitHub** → sign in → select your factory repo → save. That's it — no keys, no settings pages, no jargon; every button in the ceremony is named in [`SETUP-PATH.md`](SETUP-PATH.md) §C. (Fine-grained tokens exist as an upgrade for factories that outgrow this someday — most never do; see [`tokens/TOKEN-MODEL.md`](../tokens/TOKEN-MODEL.md) when that day comes, not before.) Second, the **skills upload** for `team-leader` — Team Leader's card, named for the seat (Settings → Skills). It offers the Coach card for your Chat room at the same time — plain-English explanations of anything, whenever you want them.
- Team Leader boots in the Cowork room, reads the repo **itself** — and proves it, by reading your journal back to you aloud: what the factory did, in its own words. Until a seat proves it can read, it hasn't connected; "should work" doesn't count here (RULE 1 binds the seats, not you). If you want the proof to go one step further, Team Leader dictates a one-line journal update and **Engineer opens the PR on Team Leader's words** — the chat seats see and propose; Engineer holds the pen.
- **The handoff, worth celebrating on its own:** from this stage on, Team Leader is the centre — it plans, specs, and audits; Engineer falls back to what it does best: building. Engineer was your guide for these first stages *by design* — the seat that connects to your repo effortlessly is the right one to stand the others up — and handing you to Team Leader is it finishing that job, not leaving you. This is the moment the factory starts *running* instead of being installed.

*Done looks like: Team Leader reads your journal back to you; the loop is alive.*

## Stage 3 — The surfacer 🎉

*The lightest seat, for the room you already live in.*

- One skill upload for the Chat room: Coach — the seat that briefs you, researches, and recommends, without ever holding the pen on plans. Useful precisely because it's light.

*Done looks like: Coach boots in Chat and gives you a one-paragraph state-of-the-factory that matches reality.*

## Stage 4 — The design lane (optional) 🎉

*Only if your missions have a visual side — most do eventually.*

- Claude Design gets a project, and every Creative Director session boots the same one-time way the other card-booted seats do: add the `creative-director` skill via the **canvas skill picker** (live as of 2026-07-23) and invoke it by name. (You can still point the seat at its boot file, [`seats/designer/BOOT-PROMPT.md`](../seats/designer/BOOT-PROMPT.md), by pasting it or a one-line pointer at the top of the session — the committed file is the mechanism; anything dropped loose at the project's root is a convenience that may not survive into a fresh session.) Bring whatever brand material exists — real assets beat perfect assets, and modest ones are enough to start (the [design-guide mission](../missions/design-guide/MISSION.md) is built for exactly that).
- Creative Director's deliverables reach the factory through you: the design lane executes the visual work, Engineer lands it by PR, you merge. Same gate as everything — Creative Director and Engineer are both execution lanes; the job picks which one (often both).

*Done looks like: one real design deliverable through the full loop.*

## Stage 5 — Eyes and dispatch: the two tools (optional) 🎓

*The last two tools, for when the work leaves the repo — things the team uses, not more team members.*

- **Claude in Chrome — a tool, not a team member**: installed once from its official page ([`SETUP-PATH.md`](SETUP-PATH.md) §A names each dialog, including the per-site permission cards), kept off by default, opened when a live page needs real eyes — and given its one-time site permission before first use (the step is in [`inspector.md`](../specialists/inspector.md)). Your Engineer seat drives it, and every action waits on your **Allow-once** approval.
- **Dispatch — Team Leader's feature** (desktop & mobile): your orders carried to the seats, each seat's status relayed back to your phone, and research that runs while you do something else. What comes back from the field is leads, not facts, until Team Leader verifies — the factory's golden rule for anything gathered in the field (the page is [`dispatch-mobile-scout.md`](../specialists/dispatch-mobile-scout.md)).
- And the pocket loop, if you haven't already: GitHub Mobile on your phone means review-and-merge from anywhere ([`MOBILE.md`](MOBILE.md)).

*Done looks like: **graduation** — one full loop, recon → plan → build → audit → your merge → the journal remembers. From here the factory grows by working, and [`USAGE.md`](USAGE.md) is your pacing guide.* 🎓

> **Later, and only if you need it — managing more than your office.** When your factory starts looking after a second production repo (a live website, an app, a client project — a **workshop**), it manages it the same gated way it manages the office: read to audit, change only by a PR you merge, never touching `main`. That's an advanced shelf, not a stage — reach for [`../guides/WORKSHOPS.md`](../guides/WORKSHOPS.md) the day it's real, not before.

## The drift ritual (first-class, not an appendix)

Some session, some day, a seat will go off the rules — confidently wrong, forgetful, weird. This is expected, designed-for, and fixed in two moves: say **"journal out"** (the seat writes an honest entry, misses included), then **close it and boot a fresh seat** — which reads that entry and picks up clean. Never argue a drifting seat back to health; the repo is the memory, the session never was (RULE 17). The state face is the early warning the ritual watches for: a seat sliding to running-hot, or flashing a face that doesn't match the work, is the cue to run these two moves before the drift compounds.
